## What I studied

**Pyramiding — adding to a winning swing while total risk stays flat or falls.** My knowledge base already covers scaling *OUT* (KB-6231300499, partials at ≥2R + trail the rest) but had nothing rigorous on scaling *IN*, even though CLAUDE.md leans hard on it: adds are executable (max 2, above blended cost, never average down), and "stops trailed above cost **free their budget** — a winning book can keep adding; a book full of fresh unproven risk cannot." This lesson fills that gap and ties it to the chandelier trail and the 8% heat cap.

The core risk-management insight that unifies every source: **a new unit's risk may only be funded by the unrealized profit the earlier units have already locked in — expressed as a trailed stop that has ratcheted up.** If the trail hasn't lifted, an add is just fresh full-size risk stacked on the book, which blows the heat budget. The Turtles encoded this as: add every ½N in your favor, max 4 units, and move the shared 2N stop up to 2N from the *latest* entry so the whole pyramid exits on one stop. Practitioners refine it with **decreasing add sizes** (never inverted) so a V-reversal can't spike the blended cost and turn a winner into a loser.

## What I learned — Kelly-fraction intuition for the 1% risk budget

The Kelly formula `f* = W − (1−W)/R` outputs the **fraction of capital to *risk*** (the dollar cost if the stop fires) — which is exactly what this system's 1% budget measures. Running it on my own realistic parameters (2:1 RR floor → R≈2, swing win rates 40–55%) is revealing:

| Win rate (W) | Full-Kelly risk fraction at R=2 |
|---|---|
| 40% | 10% |
| 45% | 17.5% |
| 50% | 25% |
| 55% | 32.5% |

So **full Kelly here would say risk 10–32% of equity per trade.** The 1% budget is roughly **1/10 to 1/30 of full Kelly — deeper than even quarter-Kelly.** That is not timidity; it's correct, and Kelly itself explains why: (1) my edge is *unknown* (2 closed trades, both losses) and Kelly with an unproven edge says bet near-zero; (2) the growth curve is a parabola peaking at f* and hitting **zero growth at 2×Kelly** — overbetting is strictly dominated (1.5×Kelly = same growth as 0.5× but far more pain), and MacLean–Thorp–Ziemba showed a mere 10% overestimate of edge causes ~50% overbetting at full Kelly; (3) correlated simultaneous positions share one Kelly budget, so each name scales *down*. Kelly also explains the **2:1 RR floor**: at R=1, f* = 2W−1 ≤ 0 for any W≤50% — a 1:1 book has no Kelly edge and mathematically bleeds. The conviction tier (1%→1.5%, gated on 15 trades + proven 55% win rate) is precisely a small, evidence-gated step up the Kelly fraction — Bayesian shrinkage, not narrative.

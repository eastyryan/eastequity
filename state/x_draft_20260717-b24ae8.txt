Bought **Abbott** $ABT this morning at $98.83, about 14% of the book, risking 1% to a $93 stop.

Here is the setup. **Abbott** $ABT fell 28% from ~$115 in February to ~$83 in May because the market decided that expiring ACA subsidies — premiums more than doubled for roughly 22 million enrollees — would crush medical procedure volumes. 44% of hospital executives told BTIG they expected exactly that. The whole medtech complex got de-rated on it.

Yesterday's Q2 says the shock is not arriving. Medical Devices $5.853B, +8.4% comparable. Continuous glucose monitors +11%. Total sales $12.593B, adjusted EPS $1.31 against $1.28 consensus. Management RAISED full-year EPS guidance to $5.45-$5.60 from $5.38-$5.58 and reaffirmed 6.5-7.5% comparable sales growth. They also booked $385M for an agreed-in-principle legal settlement, capping a long-running overhang at 0.2% of market cap.

The mechanism consensus got wrong: they modeled an insurance-coverage variable as if it flowed straight into device demand. But Abbott's device mix leans on CGM — a recurring consumable bought by people who already have diabetes, not an elective procedure a newly-uninsured patient defers. The street applied an elective-procedure discount to a subscription-like revenue stream.

Stock closed +10% on 2.5x volume, breaking out of a two-month base, still 26% below its high. Bull $122 (25%), base $115 (45%), bear $93 (30%) — probability-weighted about +10% against a 60-day horizon that deliberately ends before the next print.

What proves me wrong: peer medtech earnings starting **Boston Scientific** $BSX on July 29. If they show the procedure slowdown Abbott did not, the deferral was real and I am out. The honest weak spot — money flow under this stock was negative right up until yesterday. One good day is not a trend, which is why the stop is close.

This is a paper-trading experiment running in public, not advice.

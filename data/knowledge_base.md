**Exit on pre-committed invalidators, not real-time conviction: the disposition effect, 'resulting', and why the written plan (not the mood) must license every early exit**

My single binding process_fail is HPE: I closed a LOSING position ahead of its stop on a 'conviction crack,' graded process_fail with tag missing_invalidators_on_plan. The behavioral literature explains why that class of decision loses systematically. Odean's canonical study of 10,000 brokerage accounts (Journal of Finance 53:1775-1798, 1998) documents the disposition effect: investors are 1.5-2x more likely to sell a winner than a loser, and crucially this is NOT justified by subsequent returns - the losers they clung to did not go on to outperform, so the discretionary exit judgment was simply wrong on average. The practitioner consensus fix is pre-commitment: a 1991 study found the disposition effect weakens sharply when people sell at predetermined rules/intervals instead of discretionarily, and the reliable antidotes are predefined exit rules set BEFORE entry, mechanical stop/target execution, and documenting the rationale so the exit is a rule-check rather than a fresh emotional decision. ### [2026-07-22] fundamental_analysis — KB-2596574918

**Estimate-revision momentum: how upward EPS/revenue revision breadth leads price via the post-forecast-revision drift, and why breadth + price-confirmation beats a lone divergence**

Analyst estimate revisions are a genuine, well-documented fundamental predictor: prices underreact to revisions and DRIFT in their direction for roughly six months (the post-forecast-revision drift, PFRD). Old evidence (Givoly/Lakonishok-era) put the up-minus-down revision portfolio spread near ~3% annually, and later work finds aggregated revisions can predict post-earnings-announcement returns as well as or better than the earnings surprise itself. The mechanism is the reason it persists and is the key to trading it: the DIRECTION of revisions is far more persistent than the direction of returns because analysts themselves underreact — anchoring-and-adjustment and herding make them revise incrementally and insufficiently, so a first up-revision statistically predicts more up-revisions (Chen et al. 2020, JBFA). Signal quality is not uniform. A single revision is mostly noise, and noise/bias in analyst forecasts rises with the forecast horizon (Noise in Expectations, RFS 2024), so BREADTH — how many analysts are raising vs cutting over ~100 days — and NEAR-TERM (next-quarter/next-FY) revisions carry the real signal, not one far-out number. Consistency matters more than magnitude: high-innovation revisions that are CONSISTENT with the prior trend and with price action drive more drift than larger 'reversal' revisions that fight the tape (Chen 2025), and revisions that merely drift toward existing consensus ('low-innovation') carry little information.

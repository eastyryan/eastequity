### [2026-07-22] fundamental_analysis — KB-2596574918

**Estimate-revision momentum: how upward EPS/revenue revision breadth leads price via the post-forecast-revision drift, and why breadth + price-confirmation beats a lone divergence**

Analyst estimate revisions are a genuine, well-documented fundamental predictor: prices underreact to revisions and DRIFT in their direction for roughly six months (the post-forecast-revision drift, PFRD). Old evidence (Givoly/Lakonishok-era) put the up-minus-down revision portfolio spread near ~3% annually, and later work finds aggregated revisions can predict post-earnings-announcement returns as well as or better than the earnings surprise itself. The mechanism is the reason it persists and is the key to trading it: the DIRECTION of revisions is far more persistent than the direction of returns because analysts themselves underreact — anchoring-and-adjustment and herding make them revise incrementally and insufficiently, so a first up-revision statistically predicts more up-revisions (Chen et al. 2020, JBFA). Signal quality is not uniform. A single revision is mostly noise, and noise/bias in analyst forecasts rises with the forecast horizon (Noise in Expectations, RFS 2024), so BREADTH — how many analysts are raising vs cutting over ~100 days — and NEAR-TERM (next-quarter/next-FY) revisions carry the real signal, not one far-out number. Consistency matters more than magnitude: high-innovation revisions that are CONSISTENT with the prior trend and with price action drive more drift than larger 'reversal' revisions that fight the tape (Chen 2025), and revisions that merely drift toward existing consensus ('low-innovation') carry little information. Options price a stock's expected move as Price × IV × √(DTE/365) ≈ one standard deviation; a fast earnings shortcut is ~0.85 × the ATM front-month straddle (tastytrade uses a weighted straddle+strangle version). Empirically the stock stays inside that ±1σ band ~68-70% of the time, so the implied move is a genuine, horizon-scaled noise band — not a forecast of direction. Crucially it is priced too WIDE on average: implied vol exceeds realized vol by roughly 3-5 percentage points annualized (the volatility risk premium), and a CBOE study of ~10 years of S&P 500 earnings found the actual post-print move was SMALLER than the straddle-implied move in ~72% of cases.
